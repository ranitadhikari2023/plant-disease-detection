    About Plant Disease Detection V2

    Hierarchical Computer Vision Architecture for High-Consequence Agricultural Foliar Pathology

    1. Two-Tier Cascaded Architecture

    Standard monolithic classification models frequently exhibit cross-crop pathology confusion (for example, confusing Potato Early Blight with Tomato Early Blight). LeafScan V2 implements an explicit hierarchical cascade:

    • Tier 1 — Botanist Species Classifier: A dedicated MobileNetV2 network that identifies whether the foliar specimen is Solanum tuberosum (Potato) or Solanum lycopersicum (Tomato).
    • Tier 2 — Crop Pathology Specialists: Two decoupled specialist networks (Potato Doctor CBAM and Tomato Doctor CBAM) trained exclusively on pathology within their biological host crop.

    2. Convolutional Block Attention Module (CBAM)

    Agricultural disease lesions (such as early Phytophthora water-soaking or Alternaria target spots) often occupy less than 5% of the foliar surface area. LeafScan injects a 206,258-parameter CBAM submodule into the terminal $7 \times 7 \times 1280$ feature maps of MobileNetV2:

    • Channel Attention (206,160 parameters): Explores inter-channel relationships via spatial average and max pooling combined with a two-layer shared MLP (reduction ratio $r=16$) to amplify necrotic spectral bands.
    • Spatial Attention (98 parameters): Inter-spatial convolution ($7 \times 7$ kernel) over channel-pooled slices to localize necrotic lesions across the lamina regardless of scale.

    3. Dual Confidence Safety Gates

    To prevent dangerous or costly automated misdiagnoses, the backend enforces automated safety boundaries:

    Safety Gate Threshold Behavior when Triggered
    Gate 1: Species Confidence 90.0% Abstains from disease classification. Suppresses chemical advice; requests clearer foliage photo.
    Gate 2: Late Blight Escalation 90.0% Flags Phytophthora infestans uncertainty. Suppresses premature systemic fungicide sprays; recommends isolation and 24–48h field re-scouting.
    General Disease Confidence 70.0% Labels diagnosis as uncertain; advises physical field scouting prior to action.

    4. ONNX Runtime Edge Deployment

    All production V2 checkpoints are compiled into static Opset 17 computational graphs (models/onnx/v2/*.onnx). In verified CPU benchmark evaluations, ONNX Runtime execution demonstrated a 52.6× to 59.2× speedup over native Python graphs, reducing per-image CPU inference from ~95 ms down to 1.58 – 1.89 ms with strict numerical probability parity (maximum deviation: 1.67 × 10-6).

    6. Operational Limitations

    Laboratory vs. Field Conditions: These deep learning models were trained and benchmarked on standardized laboratory imagery. Real-world agronomic conditions (dew, dust, mixed soil reflections, pesticide residues, complex field lighting) may reduce accuracy.

    Decision Support Only: Predictions are automated screening recommendations intended for educational and decision-support purposes. They do NOT replace certified on-site plant pathology inspection or chemical regulatory labels.
